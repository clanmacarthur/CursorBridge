# Modular Gardens — Full Human Language and Terminology Canon

**Status:** mandatory project communication rule  
**Applies to:** chat responses, pitchdecks, Notion pages, builder briefs, repository handovers, definitions, diagrams, change logs and agent-to-agent transfers  
**Emergency safe word:** `FULL HUMAN`

## 1. Purpose

This canon prevents a repeated failure pattern: translating “basic,” “plain language,” “human words,” “customer-facing” or “layman’s terms” into reduced information.

The project requires complete explanations in language suited to the reader. Vocabulary may change. Coverage must not silently shrink.

## 2. Safe word

When the founder writes:

```text
FULL HUMAN
```

all output must immediately follow this canon.

The safe word means:

- no compression;
- no summary substitution;
- no missing reasoning;
- no one-line explanations for material changes;
- no silent terminology changes;
- no silent architecture, layout or scope decisions;
- define specialist terms when they first appear;
- explain why each decision was made;
- distinguish source fact, founder ruling, working proposal, inference and unresolved question;
- preserve the full project architecture;
- present the same subject in human, working and technical language where useful.

`FULL HUMAN` is an emergency override. The canon is the default for all Modular Gardens project work, even when the safe word is not repeated.

## 3. Words that must not reduce coverage

The following words control language, audience or entry point. They do **not** authorise shortening:

- Basic
- layman / layperson
- human words
- plain language
- simple language
- non-technical
- customer-facing
- public-facing
- introductory
- accessible
- explain it plainly
- explain it to a new reader

### Correct interpretation

**Basic** means a full explanation in ordinary human language.

**Plain language** means translate jargon, not delete meaning.

**Customer-facing** means organise the information around the customer’s decisions and needs, not remove the underlying proposition.

**Non-technical** means explain the machinery through ordinary examples and definitions, not conceal the machinery.

## 4. Words that may reduce length

Length may be reduced only when the founder explicitly uses a reduction instruction such as:

- shorten;
- trim;
- condense;
- summarise;
- one paragraph;
- maximum word count;
- bullet-only;
- headline-only;
- executive summary.

Even then, the response must state what has been omitted when omission could affect meaning or later decisions.

## 5. Required output depths

### Basic — full human explanation

Basic is not a summary.

It must:

- explain the whole subject from beginning to end;
- define unfamiliar terms;
- explain purpose, relationships, stages and consequences;
- use practical examples;
- show what the reader can do;
- include readable diagrams when they improve understanding;
- state what is established and what is still open;
- fill the available page with useful content rather than leaving a token paragraph.

### Intermediate — full working explanation

Intermediate must explain:

- participants;
- responsibilities;
- stages;
- routes;
- decisions;
- records;
- permissions;
- dependencies;
- milestones;
- operating examples;
- handoffs;
- failure and recovery paths.

### Advanced — full technical architecture

Advanced must expose the complete architecture where relevant, including:

- entities and relationships;
- schemas and data contracts;
- state machines;
- provenance and evidence;
- AI boundaries;
- APIs and connectors;
- permissions and governance;
- versioning and rollback;
- repository boundaries;
- implementation status;
- unresolved technical decisions;
- acceptance tests.

Advanced is not Intermediate plus one jargon paragraph.

## 6. Multi-language output rule

Where a subject is important or complex, provide several language states in the same output:

1. **Human definition** — full explanation in ordinary language.
2. **Working definition** — operational explanation with roles, stages and records.
3. **Technical definition** — exact domain, computer, AI, scientific or governance terminology.
4. **Dictionary notes** — definitions of terms that may be unfamiliar.

These layers must describe the same accepted project, not different projects.

## 7. Dictionary and thesaurus rule

Every important project term must have a controlled entry containing:

- canonical term;
- full human definition;
- working definition;
- technical definition;
- synonyms and legacy names;
- terms it must not be confused with;
- source or founder ruling;
- status: locked, working, unresolved or deprecated;
- examples;
- affected pitchdeck tabs, Notion pages and repositories.

When a term is not defined, do not improvise a definition.

Instead:

1. identify the missing definition;
2. locate the source material;
3. state the conflict or gap;
4. identify whose ruling is required;
5. place the item in the Modular Gardens Admin decision register;
6. place a properly explained open decision in the relevant pitchdeck tab when public input is appropriate;
7. record the final ruling in this canon and the change history.

## 8. No under-the-hood decisions

Any material change must be explained before or with the output.

This includes:

- removing a term;
- adding a term;
- merging two concepts;
- splitting one concept;
- renaming a tab;
- hiding a tab;
- changing Basic/Intermediate/Advanced content;
- changing a Lens;
- changing navigation;
- changing layout;
- changing source priority;
- changing public versus internal status.

The explanation must state:

1. what changed;
2. why it changed;
3. the source or founder instruction;
4. what was preserved;
5. what was not changed;
6. what remains unresolved;
7. where the change is logged;
8. how to revert it.

One sentence is not sufficient for a material change.

## 9. Terminology closure rule

Loose terminology must not remain indefinitely.

For every disputed or undefined term, record:

- the exact problem;
- competing definitions;
- source files;
- founder’s current meaning;
- decision owner;
- required evidence;
- action required now;
- blocking dependency, when any;
- deadline or next review point;
- final destination in pitchdeck, Notion and repository documentation.

Never write “until the terminology is reconciled” without explaining:

- who performs the reconciliation;
- which sources are compared;
- what decision must be made;
- where the decision is recorded;
- what happens after the ruling.

## 10. Modular Gardens and Living Modules ruling

### Current canonical rule

**Modular Gardens is the single public project, system and platform name.**

**Living Modules is retained as a legacy and descriptive source term for physical living units or installations within Modular Gardens. It is not a separate public project, route, platform or competing product.**

Therefore:

- do not create separate Modular Gardens and Living Modules branches;
- do not use `MG/LM` in public material;
- do not create a public tab named `MG/LM`;
- do not describe Living Modules as a separate HNOS route unless the founder explicitly reverses this ruling;
- preserve older Living Modules source material and map it into the correct Modular Gardens product, installation and historical records;
- use “Living Module” only where it genuinely helps describe a physical unit, and define it at first use.

### Human definition

Modular Gardens is the whole project. It includes the boxes, crates, living installations, botanical planning, guilds, customer platform, monitoring and wider applications. Older work sometimes called the physical installations “Living Modules.” That material remains part of Modular Gardens rather than becoming a separate project.

### Working definition

A Modular Gardens customer, steward, school, institution or site partner uses one project and one platform. The platform may generate a box, crate, wall, roof, floating system, nursery unit, habitat installation or larger site arrangement. Historical records may call an individual physical arrangement a Living Module, but the system remains Modular Gardens.

### Technical definition

`ModularGardens` is the canonical project and domain boundary. `LivingModule` may remain a subordinate physical-configuration entity or legacy alias in source mappings. It must not become a sibling domain, route or application without an explicit new founder ruling and migration record.

## 11. Pitchdeck language rules

Pitchdeck copy must:

- be self-contained for a new reader;
- never refer to the conversation, corrections or arguments;
- never say “as discussed,” “you asked,” “we changed,” or similar process language in public content;
- define specialist terms;
- explain open decisions with enough context for informed input;
- avoid internal shorthand;
- avoid one-line placeholders presented as explanations;
- separate customer, partner and admin surfaces;
- keep Basic fully informative;
- show technical detail in Advanced without hiding the human explanation.

## 12. Pitchdeck layout rules

- Never use full viewport width for ordinary body text.
- Use a centred reading column or two-/three-column composition.
- Use diagrams at readable size.
- Do not show diagram collages as the primary explanation.
- Use one logical diagram at a time or a clearly ordered vertical sequence.
- Prefer vertical progression over horizontal panning.
- Avoid nested horizontal scrollbars.
- Do not alter approved layouts outside the explicitly authorised region.
- Do not create a new selector merely because a new category is mentioned.
- Roles, pillars, subjects and system layers remain content unless explicitly authorised as controls.

## 13. Source and certainty labels

Every substantive claim must be classified internally as one of:

- founder ruling;
- source-backed definition;
- implemented;
- verified working prototype;
- specified;
- planned;
- experimental;
- proposed;
- unresolved;
- deprecated.

Public outputs must not present proposed, experimental or planned material as implemented fact.

## 14. Mandatory pre-output check

Before delivering any substantial Modular Gardens output, verify:

1. Am I translating language or deleting information?
2. Have I defined every unfamiliar term?
3. Have I explained every material decision and reason?
4. Have I silently separated, merged or renamed anything?
5. Have I preserved the approved source architecture?
6. Have I distinguished source, inference, proposal and ruling?
7. Have I used full human language rather than token summaries?
8. Have I avoided full-width body text and default layout drift?
9. Have I recorded unresolved terminology with owner and next action?
10. Have I logged the change and preserved the previous version?

If any answer is no, the output is not ready.

## 15. Required response pattern for important corrections

### Result

State the actual conclusion or deliverable.

### Why this happened

Explain the cause plainly and completely. Do not blame the founder’s wording when the model misinterpreted it.

### Canonical ruling

State the terminology, architecture or process rule now in force.

### What changes

List the concrete affected pages, tabs, files, labels and behaviours.

### What remains open

For every open point, state owner, evidence needed, next action and destination.

### Record

Link the Notion, repository and artifact records.

## 16. Founder correction priority

A direct founder correction overrides an inferred model interpretation.

When the founder says two concepts are the same, must not be separated, or must use one name, stop the existing flow and update the terminology canon before producing further public material.

Do not continue with an old inferred architecture merely because it appears in a newer document.
