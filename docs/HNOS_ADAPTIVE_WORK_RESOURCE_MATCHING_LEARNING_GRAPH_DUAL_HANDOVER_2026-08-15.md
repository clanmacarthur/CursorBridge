Use the attached/current HNOS architecture material from P0.5–P0.6 as a **proposed architecture delta for reconciliation**.

Reconcile it against the current HNOS Constitutional Trust and Sovereign Exchange architecture, current HNOS source-of-truth files, Synergiser architecture, STSH boundaries, layered mesh, Validation Workbench boundaries, contribution/value logic, and existing role/governance rules.

The proposed delta includes:

- recursive/composable lenses;
- work-defined roles rather than role-defined work;
- project/problem/business-plan decomposition into actions and dependencies;
- requirements matched against declared offers;
- users pre-declaring skills, resources, availability, conditions, permissions and start windows;
- standing bounded mandates that can permit automatic assignment when all criteria are satisfied;
- Human Synergiser retained as a first-class function;
- AI Synergiser supporting detection, comparison, simulation and routing;
- Master Synergising treated provisionally as an observed capability pattern, not yet a formal rank;
- recording human approaches to design, innovation, problem solving and opportunity discovery;
- AI learning from governed, evidenced human work tracks;
- completed work producing observed tracks;
- repeated validated tracks becoming benchmarks and reusable templates;
- applications potentially emerging from mature recurring tracks rather than every workflow beginning as an app;
- governed knowledge/data packets travelling through strict permission, provenance, validation and value paths;
- x402 / HTTP 402 used only as an optional payment adaptor for eligible governed microtransactions;
- separate treatment of permission, payment, contribution, ownership and settlement;
- STSH operating as a live solution-development and learning network, not merely a technology catalogue;
- skills, knowledge, land, equipment, materials, compute, institutional capacity and capital represented as typed offers against typed requirements;
- investors able to declare capital/resources and conditions in the same matching framework without treating capital as equivalent to labour or knowledge;
- Programme as a persistent container for multiple connected projects, ventures, assets, investment objects and work tracks;
- Living Venture lifecycle covering discovery, planning, design, validation, resourcing, building, launch, operation, maintenance, measurement, adaptation, restructuring, expansion, replication and exit;
- intervention triggers that reactivate relevant lenses when a live business/project changes or begins failing;
- multiple investment layers inside one wider programme;
- optional smart contracts/blockchain only after underlying legal rights, agreements, permissions and ownership structures are defined;
- the 3D Eco-Home / Shared Printer Programme as the main architecture stress-test.

# B. HNOS Pitchdeck Architecture Handover

Use the current HNOS architecture material from P0.5–P0.6 as new conceptual input for the existing pitchdeck.

This is a presentation and visual-communication task, not permission to redefine or implement the underlying HNOS architecture.

The central pitchdeck idea is:

**Roles do not define the work. The work reveals the roles, capabilities, resources and relationships required to complete it.**

Show HNOS beginning with a problem, objective, opportunity or plan.

Then visually show:

problem / objective / opportunity  
→ lenses activate  
→ work is decomposed into actions  
→ requirements become visible  
→ matching skills/resources/knowledge/capital can enter  
→ people, organisations, AI, technology and resources take on the required work  
→ work is completed  
→ evidence remains  
→ the travelled route becomes an observed track  
→ repeated validated tracks become benchmarks  
→ benchmarks can become reusable templates  
→ future projects begin with stronger evidence while remaining adaptable.

Use the existing faded-lens concept.

At the beginning, many possible lenses/routes should remain visually faded.

A lens or route becomes active only when the project actually triggers it.

When work finishes, the temporary participants can visually fade while the evidenced route remains.

A later comparable project should show the previous route faintly beneath the new work as a benchmark rather than a compulsory workflow.

Keep Human Synergisers visible.

The Human Synergiser is not merely an approver or simple matcher.

Show three levels of synergising activity conceptually:

- matching an existing requirement with an existing offer;
- cross-linking several complementary projects/resources;
- generative synergising: seeing that several apparently separate ideas, resources, people or opportunities can be stacked into a new proposition.

Do not promote “Master Synergiser” as a formal rank.

Present it only as a provisional example of an advanced demonstrated capability: someone able to identify, construct and stack multi-domain opportunities.

Show that HNOS can record different human approaches to:

- design;
- innovation;
- problem solving;
- business creation;
- resourcefulness;
- ecological symbiosis;
- commercial symbiosis;
- opportunity discovery.

These evidenced approaches can later inform AI-assisted problem solving and learning without removing the human Synergiser function.

Show that some HNOS applications can be understood as mature recurring tracks given dedicated interfaces.

Examples can include:

- Validation Workbench as a recurring validation/research track;
- STSH as a recurring technology/solution-development track;
- Seed & Plant Share as a recurring botanical exchange track.

Do not claim that every application literally emerged this way unless source material confirms it. Use this as the conceptual relationship.

Show that HNOS supports work beyond launch.

A project or business remains live through:

discovery  
→ planning  
→ design  
→ validation  
→ resourcing  
→ building  
→ launch  
→ operation  
→ maintenance  
→ measurement  
→ adaptation  
→ restructuring  
→ expansion / replication / exit.

Relevant lenses can reactivate when conditions change.

Examples:

- revenue decline activates financial review;
- maintenance problems activate operations/maintenance;
- new technology activates STSH;
- repeated waste streams activate circularity/synergy;
- repeated milestone failure activates a reform/restructure route.

Present this as a **Living Venture** concept.

Also show that users and organisations can pre-declare:

- skills;
- expertise;
- available time;
- capital;
- land;
- equipment;
- datasets;
- fabrication capacity;
- materials;
- institutional capacity;
- other resources.

When project requirements align with those declared offers, HNOS can propose or, where a valid pre-authorised mandate exists, automatically reserve/assign the work under preset conditions.

Use the 3D Eco-Home / Shared Printer Programme as a worked example, not as the definition of HNOS.

The example can show:

- several families sharing one larger programme;
- one family paying more to receive the first build;
- another accepting a later build in return for lower shared infrastructure cost;
- costs changing as more confirmed builds join;
- one participant contributing labour such as gardening or greenhouse work against an agreed portion of cost;
- investors funding the printer business separately from individual houses;
- separate investment routes for renewable energy, greenhouses, training or future printer operations;
- grants or charitable funding supporting specific non-return components;
- one shared programme containing multiple projects and investment objects without merging their ownership;
- the printer remaining as a productive asset after the initial houses;
- later work creating additional revenue, training or community value.

Show that HNOS can distinguish:

predicted  
→ proposed  
→ offered  
→ reserved  
→ contracted  
→ executed  
→ measured  
→ validated.

This distinction is important for investor, charity and partner confidence.

Show the value of HNOS data as:

- evidence of actual demand;
- declared resources;
- known skills;
- known constraints;
- work required;
- progress;
- cost;
- contribution;
- outcomes;
- downstream value;
- validated learning.

Do not present forecasts as guaranteed results.

For the economic layer, show governed microtransactions and value-transfer events conceptually.

Do not make blockchain or x402 the centre of the story.

Where relevant, show that HNOS can support optional programmable agreements and machine payments after underlying rights, permissions and legal agreements are established.

Do not imply:

- NFT automatically means ownership;
- blockchain automatically creates legal rights;
- payment creates permission;
- investment rights, land rights, contribution rights and project participation are the same thing.

The pitchdeck should communicate the larger proposition:

**HNOS is a living coordination system in which work reveals the organisation required, resources can slot in and out as needed, evidence accumulates through execution, successful routes become reusable knowledge, and projects can continue adapting after launch rather than ending when the original business plan is complete.**
